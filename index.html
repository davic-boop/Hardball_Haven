Flurry of Roster Moves Ahead of 40-Man Deadline

Published on November 17, 2026

Intro

Signings, and waiver claims, and DFA’s, oh my! Lots of action because of the 40-man deadlines needing to be set, but let's start with the additions and end with the subtractions so that we can get through all of it. Dustin May has agreed to a 3-year, $52.5 million pact with the Chicago White Sox, in an attempt to bolster their starting five. The Tigers officially signed the righty reliever Emilio Pagan to an $11.5 million 1-year deal. Similarly, Boston finalized the roster augmentation that is Jake Bauers’ 4-year $58 million contract. Also, while these are technicalities, both Ryan Jeffers of the Twins and David Bednar of the Yankees picked up their qualifying offers, each getting paid $23.15 million on the year.

Now for some trades! The Red Sox swapped for righty pitching prospect Kenya Huggins, sending the young left handed reliever Raymond Burgos over to the A’s. On top of that, Boston got rid of another pitcher in righty Angel Bastardo and switched him out for Kansas City’s Jack Pineda, a shortstop who bats left handed.

Next up we got some good ole’ fashion waiver claims. Funnily enough, all the players claimed came from the Orioles. The Nats claimed LHP Phillip Abner. After that Atlanta picked up RHP Cameron Foster. The Yanks then added RHP Chase Hampton. Finally, the Rangers acquired Josh White, a right handed pitcher.

DFA’s

Dun Dun Dun! It looks like these players were all unlucky enough to be removed from their teams 40-man rosters. If I am doing my math right… there's 19 unfortunate athletes who no longer have control of their own destiny, how fun! I’ll go in order of division and then record to try to keep things orderly.

The AL East had nothing to do with this, so I’ll skip straight to the Central. The White Sox got us started when they removed catcher Joey Bart. After that, the Tigers cut loose 4 players: 3B Jace Jung, LHP Jake Miller, SS Trey Sweeny, and veteran CF Matt Vierling. Kansas City also hopped in on the festivities when they did it to RHP Nick Mears.

The West was mostly innocent, but the (now just) Athletics did DFA 3 of their RHP: Hayden Juenger, Yunior Tur, and Braden Nett.

The NL East was active, but oddly only the two worst teams in it. The Nationals casted off 4 pitchers: Righties Gus Varland, Braxton Roxby, and Josiah Gray, and then LHP Carson Palmquist. The Mets ridded themselves of just one player, a right handed pitcher, Dylan Ross.

The St. Louis Cardinals took it upon themselves to keep it going in the NL Central, but only DFA’d RHP Max Rajcic.

As for the NL West, the Dodgers tried one-upping the Giants as per usual, but they both only let go of two players each in the end. LA cut off SS Christian Cairo and RHP Jake Cousins, while SF broke up with SS Christian Koss and RHP Rowan Wick. What. A. Doozy.

40-Man Replacements

I cannot go through all of these tonight, literally every team added multiple players to their rosters. But if you care about it that much, maybe I’ll go over it tomorrow. My other plans for the 18th are to go through most of the bigger moves in depth, some of them I like, some I don’t.

Un-Fun Fact/Sources

One man drew the shortest straw today, and his name was Rece Hinds. He got off to a ripping start in the Majors with the Reds in 2024, but has not been the same since then. After a mercifully short stint in 25, he got an even shorter and less productive shot with them this year…until he was DFA’d in May. He got picked up by the Marlin’s, played a single major league game with them, and was promptly traded to the O’s for cash. In Baltimore he actually played his best all year, unfortunately it was still only a .179/.273/.250 slashline. I would say he had the worst day today because he was straight up released. The Orioles sure don’t want him, and I am just not sure who will. Hope he can turn things around and get another chance though, he was quite the dynamic player when he first came up.

First Post! Talking Transactions

Published on November 16, 2026

Intro

It's as good a place as any to start with the most recent transactions, so what happened on that side of the game today? Well, not much exactly but, we’re in the middle of November and there might not even be a full season of ball next year so I’ll take what I can get! Today the Baltimore Orioles re-signed the 28 year-old righty shortstop José Barrero to a minor league contract after he elected free agency on Halloween earlier this year.

Barrero’s History

Barrero was signed as an amature free agent by the Cincinnati Reds in 2017 and worked his way up their system till he debuted in 2020. He was moved around a lot his first two seasons between the minors and majors, only accruing 130 AB’s in that time but also -0.6 bWAR. In 2023 he was finally given a longer shot at it and… blew it. In 48 games he was worth -1.6 bWAR and in 2023 he had a similar amount of time with only slightly better results and was subsequently put on waivers.

Spring of 2024 the Rangers picked him up and he played with AAA but he never made it to the majors with them and elected free agency in November. After that he was picked up by the Cardinals, and played with their minors till the end of April 2025 when he was called back up to the bigs. He played 22 games for St. Louis and posted a rough .138/.194/.276 slash-line, later being DFA’d in June. From there, he was picked up by the O’s in July and played a 2 game stint with them in the majors in September 2026. He then elected free agency, which leads us back till today.

Barrero’s Game

He has never been more than a significantly below average hitter unfortunately, and at his worst posted a -21 BRV (not qualified) on the year. He has shown alright plate discipline at times, and can draw a walk, but it is not enough to overcome his anemic batting averages. He has a tall, athletic build standing at 6’4 and 211 lbs and has been a serviceable defender at short. In his more complete seasons he has had a significantly above average arm (82 percentile) and a slightly below average range.

Outlook

This is one of those signings that just happens because teams need guys in the minors that can play ball if needed. Minor Leaguers make the world go ‘round after all! There’s not much offensive upside, but if he can start getting on base more than he is now, he could be a below average bench piece. That guy on your team they put in when your starter needs a day and you go, “oh yeah, forgot he played for us”. There is not much risk in this for the O’s but also not much to gain, so most likely do not expect any big news coming from this move.

Fun Fact / Sources

Our guy José Barrero also played under the name José Garcia! I cannot see when his name was changed but I feel like it's a bit out of the norm to see a name change nowadays.
